# 0.1 Dynamic town economy

## Scope and dependencies

This line adds a deterministic, bounded daily market planner for households, employers and shops. It consumes current account cash, inventory, wage-settlement and transport quotes as read-only snapshots. The existing `city_core::transaction::Engine` remains the only authority for cash and goods. Existing `Timeline` payroll and tax/debt operations remain the authority for their respective settlements. `economy::Economy` (PE0/PE1) is a 30-day, four-role laboratory with fixed corridors and fixed offers; it is a comparison fixture, not the dynamic mechanism.

No independent balance, inventory, clock or persistence store is introduced. A market plan is identified by world/branch/revision/day and may be applied at most once by the existing transaction journal. If application fails, its proposals are discarded and the next plan is derived from a fresh authoritative snapshot. Its arithmetic is checked integer arithmetic.

## Inputs and outputs

Each day the planner reads bounded household need rows, employers, shops, recipe inputs, inventory snapshots, account cash snapshots, wage accrual totals, and per-store delivery quotes. A household account may have need rows for several goods; all rows share and debit its one authoritative spendable balance in stable need-ID order. A quote has an explicit reachable flag, route/source identity, and marginal cost. Missing or unreachable quotes mean no delivery; geometric proximity is not treated as access. Household cash and wage accrual are read from the Engine/Timeline for the same day. The planner never issues a second wage payment. Recipe workers are explicitly allocated among an employer's recipes and cannot exceed that employer's available workforce. `wage_cost_per_worker_day` is Timeline-accrued cost used in COGS; `payroll_paid_today` is reporting data and causes no second cash transfer.

Household groups retain three different quantities: `reference_need` is the natural/baseline need; `market_demand` is the elasticity-adjusted amount at the cheapest currently executable offer (it must have stock and remaining route capacity); and `actual_sales` is what the plan can fulfill. `unmet_need = reference_need - actual_sales`, so high prices never silently make natural need disappear. `unmet_market_demand` is the executable-price demand left unpurchased. As the planner walks offers in increasing delivered price, each offer recomputes the cumulative ceiling `min(need, need * reference_price / offer_price)` and can only buy the remaining difference between that ceiling and prior purchases. This prevents an empty cheap shop from setting demand for an expensive shop, and prevents a small amount at a cheap price from unlocking the full original need at a higher marginal price. Affordability, inventory and shared route capacity still cap each offer. Shops tie-break by delivered price, account and stable stock ID. Sales, inputs, wages, rent, utility and transport charges are cost lines, never automatically created income. Output production needs a declared recipe and is bounded by input stock, explicitly shared labor allocation, daily utility capacity, and spendable cash for operating charges. Every normalized input unit is accounted as output or declared waste; unsupported conversions are rejected. COGS uses the same integer origin-lot and offset basis slicing rule as Engine `MoveStock`.

The planner emits a bounded `SettlementBatch` containing stock inputs consumed, output lots earned, buyer/seller and fee-recipient transfers, and reconciliation totals. It does not mutate `World`. Each batch must be committed atomically through the existing transaction journal using a coordinator-supplied operation ID. If application fails, its proposals are discarded and the next plan is derived from a fresh authoritative snapshot. Engine `MoveStock` preserves one lot's basis and requires destination offsets to be contiguous; a destination inventory row cannot combine different lots. The 128-row Engine stock cap therefore bounds concurrent shop inventory lots. A successful production/market day needs an Engine primitive that checks one expected revision, cash protection, recipe quantities, actual stock lots, labor and utility caps; consumes inputs; issues a separate output lot; posts every cash transfer; updates retained basis; and writes a receipt/journal record as one atomic, idempotent operation. Current Engine actions do not provide that primitive. Until the root-coordinated Engine primitive is available, this line is a planner/API and does not claim a settled 28-day economic loop. Do not simulate Engine acceptance with a private shadow ledger.

`reconcile(input, batch)` is a read-only preflight against the exact same world, branch, revision, day and operation ID. It validates the batch by deterministic re-planning from that authoritative snapshot, then audits each cash payer/receiver leg, named fee beneficiary, input/output/waste quantity, quote and recipe identity, worker-days, shared utility quantity, and basis equation. Lot basis is re-derived from the immutable origin quantity/basis and contiguous offset using the same integer floor rule, so each slice and final remainder must match. Unknown lot, account, recipe or route IDs, duplicate account/stock/quote/recipe IDs, altered payees or amounts, and stale revision/day all reject. Protected account cash remains unavailable to production and household purchases. Recipes assigned to the same employer share its worker pool; recipes referencing the same utility account share one utility pool and must carry the same authoritative pool capacity. Payroll contributes to output basis from the Timeline snapshot; this batch does not debit it again. Reconciliation creates no new ledger and no state mutation; Engine must independently recheck these facts when it accepts a compact command.

Engine integration should use a compact command rather than serialize the planner's large `Vec<PlanLine>` into an Action or journal. Proposed shape for the Engine owner to adapt: `SettleMarketDay { operation_id, expected_revision, day, profile_id, scenario_id }`, with the profile/scenario resolving to bounded immutable recipe definitions and a coordinator input version. Recipe identity is a stable nonzero `recipe_id`; recipe definitions live in bounded world/profile data, not repeated in each action. The Engine derives its batch from current state and commits it once. A journal receipt should retain the compact command identity, input revision, deterministic result digest, and outcome; it must not need a duplicate local account or inventory ledger. Exact receipt recovery and no-double-apply remain Engine responsibilities.

Lot boundary: each stock slot holds one origin lot and its current contiguous offset, not a merged pile. `MoveStock`'s existing single-lot/contiguous-offset rule means cross-day store replenishment requires a free destination stock slot per incoming lot (up to the current 128 stock rows), or a separately designed Engine-owned lot table with an explicit bound. The planner cannot add a fresh production lot into an occupied output slot from another lot. It must preserve the exact original basis and remaining basis through partial sales; any mixing/weighted-average conversion needs a separately reviewed transaction invariant, never an adapter-side shortcut.

Transport adapter status: the existing `exchange::QuoteSpec`/`Quote`/`Shipment` records have stable quote IDs, seller/buyer/carrier/source/destination, resource kind, quantity, unit price, expiry, edge path, and pending/transit/delivered/canceled/expired status. They represent bounded cross-city freight, electricity and water fulfilment, with up to eight quotes and four shipments. They are not a local shop-to-household accessibility/road-capacity DTO. The current `DeliveryQuote` therefore remains a fixture-facing seam: a future adapter can use the realized delivered shipment and the ordered path/edge identities for cross-city supply, while local accessibility must come from the transport route service. No quote should be marked reachable from distance alone, and asynchronous transit must not be sold as delivered stock. The planner's route source key must remain stable for the full quote/path lifetime and share capacity across all consumers of that route/resource.

## Failure and acceptance

Missing cash, inventory, recipe input, labor, utilities, or a route reduces or rejects the corresponding plan. A failed atomic settlement leaves the authoritative Engine state unchanged; replaying the same operation id returns its recorded result instead of charging or producing twice. The planner itself is pure, so retrying against the same revision yields byte-identical output. Required propagation counterexamples are explicit: (1) stockout leaves goods payments at zero and preserves reference unmet need; (2) cash at the protected floor leaves sales at zero and emits `NoCash`; (3) a closed/unreachable route emits `MissingRoute` and no sale; (4) higher delivered fees reprice demand while reference need remains unmet; (5) if a cheap offer has no stock, an expensive offer is limited by its own price ceiling; (6) if cheap stock or route capacity runs out, the next expensive offer recomputes the cumulative demand ceiling; (7) a higher utility input cost can make an otherwise feasible recipe unaffordable, reducing output and then sales; (8) lower household count/need reduces purchases, while unsold goods remain inventory; and (9) offer input order cannot affect the plan. These are mechanism tests, not calibrated market response or proof of a balanced economy.

The acceptance scenario is 28 game days with verified wage/tax/debt settlement, production input/output conservation, household budgets, sales limited by stock and reachable delivery, and end-of-day reconciliation of every account and quantity. Fault cases include empty cash, empty inventory, closed road, high freight/energy cost, low demand, and replay after an interrupted settlement. A seeded opening balance is reported separately from earned revenue; survival on opening capital alone is not sustainable operation. Mechanism correctness and economic balance are distinct gates.

## Limits and resource budget

Initial bounded profile: at most 64 household need rows, 16 employers, 16 shops, 128 shop-lot slots, 16 recipes, 8 goods, and 1024 settlement lines/day. Need rows aggregate households; no per-household agents or continuous inference. Planning is `O((household_need_rows * shop_lots) + recipes + settlement_lines)` with checked integer operations. The input JSON cap is 128 KiB; the planner sets a 512 KiB additional-heap target for decoded bounded rows, projected lot/route/utility pools and the batch. This is a structural target only, not measured heap evidence; the integrated worker must measure peak RSS/heap and may not raise the cap without revisiting the 16 GiB budget. No persistent history is retained here. These limits and the CPU/memory estimate require measurement after the shared Engine integration.

Initial transport and utility quotes are explicit typed inputs. Any fixture quote must be labeled a test substitute. The example CLI output says `pureplan_not_settled` and `fixture_supply_not_integrated`; it is not proof that the Engine fulfilled transport or utility. No economic parameter is presented as balanced until sensitivity and sustained-operation tests have been run.
