# Bounded town-business exit planner

Status: candidate planner/API contract. It is not an Engine settlement implementation and is not legal advice or a jurisdiction-calibrated insolvency policy.

## Boundary and minimum Engine adapter

The Engine remains the only writer of accounts, Timeline claims/payroll, inventory lots and shop/production state. The exit planner accepts one immutable, same-world/branch/revision/day snapshot and returns compact proposed actions. It never edits `MarketInput`, mutates a balance, consumes a lot, creates sale proceeds, cancels a Timeline row, or pays a claim. The Engine must re-read and validate every source ID and snapshot revision, then execute accepted actions atomically through its existing transaction path. Retries use the Engine operation ID; the planner introduces no ledger, journal, clock, or persistence.

The adapter supplies: (1) the company's `AccountSnapshot` including `protected`; (2) a complete bounded page of the company's outstanding Timeline claims, including wage arrears derived from Timeline accrued-minus-paid, future due dates, source identity and a jurisdiction-approved priority tier; (3) active payroll IDs plus an explicit flag that termination/notice/severance obligations are fully represented among claims; (4) company-owned inventory rows with stable lot IDs and an explicit sale-ready flag; (5) a complete shareholder register; and (6) explicit completeness flags for claims, tax assessment, asset ownership/encumbrances, legal clearance, and exit employment terms. Rows belonging to a different company's or person's account are rejected. Timeline payroll is never paid a second time by the planner.

No priority order is inferred. A reviewed policy must enumerate the complete claim-class order (tax, wage, secured debt, unsecured debt, other) and explicitly approve the supported same-tier allocation rule (pro-rata by outstanding amount with stable-ID cent remainders); missing, partial, unsupported or unknown policy blocks claim settlement and all residual distribution. A legal-clearance state of unknown/denied, incomplete tax/claim/asset/employment snapshots, or unmodeled obligations also blocks the corresponding irreversible steps. A blocked plan may still request the safe cessation of new production; it cannot forgive claims, cancel unassessed obligations, transfer company assets to people, or draw on protected cash.

## Exit sequence

A player/authorized workflow must explicitly request exit; operating losses alone do not silently close a company. The plan then: stop new production and new input commitments; stop/cancel future payroll only when its required termination costs are fully represented; keep eligible existing stock in the normal market as liquidation-only offers (without assuming it sells); use only company spendable cash (`cash - protected`) to pay due claims in the reviewed priority order; and replan after actual market sales update the Engine snapshot. Claims within the same priority tier share insufficient cash pro rata in integer cents, with deterministic claim-ID ordering for leftover cents. Unpaid balances remain claims. Future claims, unsold inventory, unresolved tax, or a blocked legal state prevent final closure/distribution.

Residual distribution is not part of the ordinary liquidation step. It is proposed only after a fresh snapshot proves all claims are zero, tax/asset/employment/claim snapshots are complete, legal clearance permits distribution, inventory/encumbrances are resolved, and company spendable cash remains. It is proportional to registered shares; deterministic cent remainders go by ascending shareholder account ID. Protected cash stays in the company account. No personal balance is counted as company cash or vice versa. If these conditions are unavailable, remaining value stays in the company and status is blocked/waiting rather than distributed or written off.

## Bounded memory

The first adapter contract caps one plan at 64 outstanding claim rows, 64 active payroll rows, 128 inventory lots, 64 shareholders, and 260 output actions. Larger snapshots return `ExitError::Limit`; the Engine may later add deterministic paging/aggregation without changing the planner into a second ledger. The planner validates deterministic source-ID ordering within each claim class and never clones the large market snapshot. At the 260-action maximum, the output is a single bounded vector; no action contains an unbounded list. The 64 KiB estimate covers planner-owned row/output working memory only; it is not a peak-memory bound for JSON decoding or the full adapter path. In particular, `deserialize_schema` currently deserializes into an allocated `String` and checks its length afterward. Any public JSON entry point must first cap raw bytes through the existing bounded reader before calling Serde, and the complete serialized path still needs a separate measurement. The existing 512 KiB economy planner target remains unchanged.

## Implementation scope and acceptance

Owned source: `crates/city-core/src/town_economy/exit.rs`, nested export in `town_economy.rs`, and `crates/city-core/tests/town_business_exit.rs` plus bounded JSON fixtures. No changes to Engine/Store/World/transaction/assembly/Timeline, existing economy planner behavior, canonical work evidence, or shared target.

Acceptance tests must cover solvent voluntary exit; protected cash; due wage and debt priority; insufficient cash with same-tier pro-rata and deterministic remainder; unpaid/future claims remain and block distribution; inventory offers do not become proceeds until a new authoritative snapshot; no owner payout before all creditor/tax claims clear; separate company and personal balances; missing/unknown legal priority, tax, employment, asset or claim completeness blocks; duplicate identities/oversized input/overflow reject; and a fully cleared exit distributes only spendable company residual by shares. Fixtures are synthetic mechanisms, not calibrated legal rules, profitability or long-run sustainability. Cargo tests, strict Clippy and executable fixture validation require a later exclusive validation window.
